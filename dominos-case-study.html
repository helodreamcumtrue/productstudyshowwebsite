Domino's New Packaging: a strategic redesign

A teardown of Domino's eco-friendly, stackable pizza box: why the old box failed, what the redesign aims to fix, and how it could be rolled out and measured.

Problem: why the old box failed

The standard pizza box created friction in three places: it let pizza go cold, it couldn't be recycled once greasy, and it didn't hold up when stacked.

Critical

Thermodynamics

Standard corrugated cardboard has no insulation, so pizza loses its ideal serving temperature quickly during delivery.

40%Heat loss in 15 minutes
High waste

Sustainability

Grease soaks into the fibres, so a large share of legacy boxes can't be recycled in municipal facilities.

30%Unrecyclable volume
Friction

Structural integrity

Weak sidewalls limit stacking during bulk deliveries, often crushing toppings and hurting presentation.

3 boxesSafe stack limit
The core insight

The market needed a solution that balances thermal efficiency with eco-accountability.

Strategic rationale: why the change?

The shift to eco-friendly, stackable packaging for the modern delivery ecosystem rests on four business objectives aimed at long-term growth.

-30% plastic

Sustainability first

Shift to 100% recycled materials to cut the carbon footprint significantly.

Premium

Brand modernization

Lift brand perception from "fast food" to a "premium dining experience".

+15% heat

Heat retention

A new corrugated structure keeps heat 15% longer during last-mile delivery.

2x efficiency

Stackability

Optimized geometry fits standard courier bags and doubles capacity.

+24%Projected ROI, FY 2025-2026
-12.5%Carbon footprint, projected FY 2023-2025 (-2% YoY)
+8 ptsCustomer satisfaction (NPS), pre vs post launch (+15% YoY)

The box stops being packaging and becomes part of the meal, so it has to keep pizza hot and fit the planet.

From concept to counter: the rollout

A phased approach to bringing the new packaging to 1,500+ franchise locations. Phase 2 is currently active.

  1. Phase 1: Pilot program Completed

    Q1 2024

    Beta tested in 50 select stores across Tier-1 metro cities (Delhi and Mumbai pilots), focused on customer feedback about heat retention.

    50 stores98% positive feedback

  2. Phase 2: Supply chain optimization In progress

    Q2 2024

    Onboarding sustainable material vendors and retraining kitchen staff on new folding techniques, plus logistics restructuring.

    Staff trainingVendor onboarding

  3. Phase 3: Nationwide launch Upcoming

    Q3 2024

    Full rollout to all 1,500+ locations, targeting Tier-2 cities, with a major "Unboxing" marketing campaign.

The final slice: verdict

A teardown of the new sustainable aesthetic and its measurable impact on brand perception.

Objectives

Reduce material waste by 15% while improving heat retention, and modernize the brand voice without alienating the core customer base.

Design execution

A minimalist visual language using two-colour printing to lower ink costs and make recycling easier. A dark-mode aesthetic appeals to digital-native users.
